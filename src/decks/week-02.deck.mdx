# Little's Law

**SLOP3068, week 2**

Amos Redfern

---

## The claim

You can work out how long people are waiting

**without timing a single one of them**

---

{/* _class: impact */}

## L = &lambda;W

---

## What the letters are

| Symbol | What it is | How you get it |
|---|---|---|
| **L** | number in the system | count heads |
| **&lambda;** | arrival rate | count arrivals, divide by time |
| **W** | time in the system | the thing you wanted |

Two of these are easy from thirty metres away.

The third one is not, so we solve for it.

---

## Do it now

Look at the queue downstairs.

- twelve people in it
- four joined in the last minute

W = 12 / 4 = **three minutes**

You have not followed anybody.

---

## Why it is strange

It does not care about

- the order you serve people in
- how many servers there are
- the shape of the arrival distribution
- whether the thing queueing is a person, a packet or a kidney

No assumptions about any of it.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> Any system. Any discipline. Any distribution.
>
> The price is that every letter is an average.

---

## The price

**Stable.** Not filling up, not draining.

Point it at a growing backlog and it returns a number that is true and useless.

That number is a property of the snapshot, not of the queue.

---

## Failure one, instability

A passport office with a two-year backlog

- L is enormous
- &lambda; looks normal
- W comes out as two years

Correct. Also not a wait; it is a debt.

---

## Failure two, the boundary

Draw the box before you count.

- who balked and never joined
- who reneged and walked out
- who was served without joining

An arrival in one count and not the other breaks the law quietly.

---

## Failure three, the mismatch

Arrivals counted during the rush.

Residence time measured in the lull.

Both numbers correct. The ratio is fiction.
