:::warning[Read this part before week 1]
The fieldwork rules below are not administrative box-ticking, and we would ask
you to take them seriously. This course sends you out to stand next to queues
that other people are genuinely trying to use, and every site we still have
access to is one that a previous student was careful enough not to lose for
everybody else.
:::

Everything else on this page can safely wait until you actually need it.

## Fieldwork conduct

Most of this course takes place in public spaces where other people are simply
trying to get something done. There are four rules here, and unlike most things
in the course they are not up for negotiation.

**You observe, you do not participate.** Do not join a queue in order to
measure it, do not ask anyone to let you go ahead, and do not create a
disturbance to see what happens. If your measurement requires you to change
the queue, it is not a measurement.

**You count events, not people.** That means no photographs of individuals, no
video, no audio recording, no names written down and no following anybody
around. A tally sheet really is the whole instrument, and it is all you
need.

**You answer honestly when asked.** If a staff member or a member of the
public asks what you are doing, tell them. Say you are a student at Slop
University measuring how long the queue takes, and offer to stop. Every
student who has done this has been allowed to carry on, and the two who were
asked to leave left immediately, which is why the site is still available.

**You get permission for private property.** Anything indoors with staff, and
anything where you will be visibly recording for more than a few minutes.
Priya has a one-paragraph email that works; use it in week 1, not week 4.

Week 8 is the only session that involves speaking to people who are waiting,
and it has its own consent procedure on the session page.

## When your queue disappears

It happens most years. A shop closes, a desk moves, a service goes online, the
site withdraws permission. This is not your fault and it does not cost you
marks.

Tell Priya as soon as you know. There is a list of queues that have agreed to
be measured, and there is a set of archived observation data from previous
years for the case where a site fails after week 8 and there is no time to
start again. Using the archive is a documented decision, not a penalty, and it
gets written into your report.

## Extensions

Up to five days, granted by the convenor by email, reason not documented,
answered the same day. Longer than five days needs the university's standard
process.

[Ask for an extension](/extensions/) has the rest of it, including the form
that submits the request and what each piece costs if you miss the deadline
instead.

## Academic integrity

Your measurements have to be yours. This is the one thing that cannot be
delegated, shared or generated, because the whole course is built on the claim
that you personally stood next to a queue and wrote down what happened.
Fabricated observation data is the most serious form of misconduct available
in this course and it is treated as such.

Everything else is more relaxed than you might expect. Work with other people
on the arithmetic. Compare histograms. Argue about somebody else's redesign
and take their idea if it is better than yours, and say whose it was.

## On using a language model

You may use one. Say where, in a line at the end of the piece.

There is one thing it cannot do for you and it is worth naming, because
several students each year find this out late. It will produce fluent,
confident, entirely invented observation data if you let it, and it will
produce a redesign that helps everybody, which week 11 says is impossible. The
marker reads for the named loser and for the raw sheets in your appendix, and
those two things are where a generated report falls over.

## Access and adjustments

Several sessions involve standing outdoors for up to an hour and one involves
a forty-minute walk. Every one of them has a seated or indoor alternative that
carries the same material, and arranging it is routine rather than a
concession. Email Tessa in week 1 and nobody else needs to know.

Week 10 works from inquiry reports, several of which concern deaths. You can
take a report without fatalities, or take the written version and skip the
room, and you do not have to give a reason.

## Feedback

Written feedback on The Measured Queue comes back within two teaching weeks,
which puts it in your hands before Whose Time It Was is due. That ordering is
deliberate and it is why the deadlines sit where they do.
