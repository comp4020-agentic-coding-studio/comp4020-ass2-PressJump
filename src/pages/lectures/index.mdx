Lectures run for an hour a week, on Mondays. Each week makes a single claim,
and that claim is the line printed underneath the title, so you should be able
to disagree with it before you have heard a word of the lecture itself. That is
rather the point of putting it there.

The order is not a survey and it does not reshuffle, so it is worth knowing
roughly where you are going. Weeks 2 to 4 give you the arithmetic, and you will
genuinely need all three of them before week 5 means very much at all. From
there the question turns to who that arithmetic gets done to, which takes up
weeks 5 to 7, and then to what follows from having done it, which is weeks 8 to
11. Week 12 is really the week 1 argument all over again, except that by then
you have everything else attached to it.

Week 2 is the only one that carries a deck, mostly because Little's Law is
easier to show than to describe. The rest are taught on a board.

## The four results

There are four pieces of maths in this course, and this is genuinely all of
them. If that sounds like too few, that is the argument the course is
making.

- **Little's Law**, week 2. Queue length equals arrival rate times waiting
  time. It lets you measure a wait you never timed.
- **Kingman's approximation**, week 3. Waiting explodes as utilisation
  approaches one, and variability sets the multiplier.
- **Pooling**, week 4. One line into many servers beats many lines into one
  server each, at the same total capacity.
- **Ageing**, week 9. Raise a job's priority the longer it has waited, and
  starvation goes away without losing the benefit of serving short jobs first.

If you happen to have met all four of these before, this is still not a course
you have already taken, because not one of the four will answer the question we
put to you in week 6.

## Two weeks that need warning

:::warning[Week 10, crowd failure]
Works from published inquiry reports, several of which concern deaths. We stay

footage. There is a written version that carries the same material, and the
[policies page](/policies/) says how to take it. You do not have to give a
reason.
