One hour a week, Mondays. Each week makes one claim, and the claim is the line
under the title. You should be able to disagree with it before you have heard
the lecture.

The order is not a survey and it does not reshuffle. Weeks 2 to 4 are the
arithmetic, and you need them before week 5 will mean anything. Weeks 5 to 7
are about who the arithmetic gets done to. Weeks 8 to 11 are the consequences.
Week 12 is the week 1 argument again with everything attached.

Week 2 carries a deck. The rest are chalk.

## The four results

There are four pieces of maths in this course and this is all of them.

- **Little's Law**, week 2. Queue length equals arrival rate times waiting
  time. It lets you measure a wait you never timed.
- **Kingman's approximation**, week 3. Waiting explodes as utilisation
  approaches one, and variability sets the multiplier.
- **Pooling**, week 4. One line into many servers beats many lines into one
  server each, at the same total capacity.
- **Ageing**, week 9. Raise a job's priority the longer it has waited, and
  starvation goes away without losing the benefit of serving short jobs first.

If you have met all four before, this is still not a course you have taken,
because none of the four answers the question in week 6.
