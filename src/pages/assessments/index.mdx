Four pieces, and they are all the same piece of work seen from different
angles. You choose a queue in week 1, you measure it, you argue about it, you
change it, and you keep a record of every other queue you stood in while you
were doing that.

Nothing here is an exam and nothing here is a group project. Every deadline
falls on a Friday at 17:00.

<WeightBar />

## What you hand in

The weights and the dates are above. This is what each deadline actually
takes.

| Piece | What you hand in | Length |
|---|---|---|
| The Measured Queue | A report as a PDF, plus your raw observation sheets as a second file | 1500 words at most |
| Whose Time It Was | An essay as a PDF | 1200 to 1800 words |
| The Queue Journal | Eleven weekly entries and a closing note, in one PDF | 100 to 250 words an entry |
| The Redesign | A proposal as a PDF, and an eight minute defence in the week 12 field session | 2000 words at most |

Everything goes through the assessment portal on this site, named
`SLOP3068-A1-u1234567.pdf` and so on, with your own student number. Each brief
has the full requirements, the report structure and what the marker is looking
for against each criterion.

## What the weights are saying

The Redesign is worth more than everything else because it is the only piece
that asks you to be wrong in public, in advance, with the arithmetic on the
page. The Measured Queue is worth a quarter because a redesign built on bad
measurement is a guess with a diagram attached.

The Queue Journal is worth as much as the essay, which surprises people. It is
eleven weeks of small honest effort against one weekend of large effort, and
the course has a view about which of those is harder.

:::info[One queue, four angles]
All four pieces point at the queue you choose in week 1. Change your queue and
you change every piece of assessment in the course, which is why week 1 spends
two hours on the choice and why it does not move after week 2.
:::
