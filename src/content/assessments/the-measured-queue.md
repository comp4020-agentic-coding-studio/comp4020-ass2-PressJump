## The brief

> Choose a queue. Measure it until you can predict it. Then say plainly which
> of your numbers you would bet money on and which you would not.

This is the queue you keep for the whole semester. Every later piece of work
in this course points back at it, so choose one you can physically get to on
two different days without it being a project in itself. A till, a counter, a
platform, a lift lobby, a coffee cart, a help desk, a bus door. Queues you
cannot stand next to are week 7's problem, not yours yet.

The measuring is the hard part and it is hard in a specific way. You will find
that the moment you try to write down an arrival you have to decide what an
arrival is. Someone joins the line, leaves to look at a shelf, and comes back.
Two people arrive and one of them orders. A member of staff opens a second
till for ninety seconds. None of that is in the textbook and all of it is in
your data, and how you resolve it is most of what is being marked here.

Week 3 is the reason this is due in week 5 and not week 3. Averages are not
enough. A queue with a mean service time of two minutes behaves completely
differently depending on whether every customer takes two minutes or half of
them take twenty seconds and the rest take four minutes, and your report has
to show that you know which of those you are looking at.

## What you submit

A report of at most 1500 words, plus your raw observation sheets as an
appendix. The appendix does not count towards the length and it is read.

Write it for Priya, who has stood next to more queues than you have and will
notice an arrival rate that cannot be right. Numbers get the method that
produced them, in the same sentence where possible.

The last paragraph is not optional. Name one quantity you wanted and could not
get. Everybody has one. The reports that pretend otherwise are the ones that
lose marks here.
