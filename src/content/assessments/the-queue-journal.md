## The task

> Write one short entry a week, for eleven weeks, about a queue you were in or
> watched that week. Submit all of them together at the end.

This is the third of four pieces and it is worth 20 percent. You submit it
once, in week 11, but it is written across the whole semester and it is marked
on the whole semester. Set a recurring reminder in week 1.

The purpose is to build the habit of reading a queue without instruments, and
to give you eleven worked examples of your own to draw on in the other three
pieces. Entries are short by design.

## What each entry has to contain

Every entry carries a heading in this form:

`Week 4 · queue observed 12 March 2027 · written 12 March 2027`

Then 100 to 250 words covering four things:

1. **Which queue.** Where it was, what it was for, roughly how many people.
2. **What happened.** What you saw, including the discipline in operation and
   anything that broke it.
3. **One thing you could measure.** Name the quantity and the instrument. You
   do not have to measure it.
4. **One thing you could not.** Name it and say what makes it out of reach.

Points 3 and 4 are required in every entry. An entry missing either is
incomplete.

## Choosing queues

Vary them. A journal of eleven supermarket tills meets the format and scores
in the middle band, because the range of situations is the thing that makes
the habit worth having.

Most entries should be about queues you did not choose to be in: an airport, a
phone line, a half-time toilet queue, a wait for a reply to an email, a
checkout, a boarding gate, a doctor's waiting room, a traffic merge. Two or
three entries may be about the queue you measured in week 1.

No entry needs a photograph, and the fieldwork conduct rules on the
[policies page](/policies/) apply to the journal as they do to everything
else in this course.

## Weeks with no entry

Write `Week 6: no entry` and move on. A missing week stated as missing costs
less than an entry written five weeks late and presented as contemporaneous.
Do not backfill.

There is no entry for week 12. That week is the defence for The Redesign.

## The closing note

At most 300 words, at the end of the document, after the last entry. Answer
these three questions directly:

- Which entry changed most between what you noticed at the time and what you
  would notice now?
- Which piece of course vocabulary shows up first in your entries, and in
  which week?
- Which queue in the journal would you most want to redesign, and why did you
  not choose it for The Redesign?

The closing note is not a summary of the entries. The marker has just read
them.

## What to submit

One PDF, through the assessment portal on this site, by 17:00 Friday of
week 11. Name it `SLOP3068-A3-u1234567.pdf`.

Entries in week order, week 1 first. Start each entry on its own paragraph
with the heading above. Put your student number on the first page. There is no
overall word limit; the per-entry limits do the work.

## How the bands work

This piece is marked holistically, as one document. The band descriptions are
in the panel below this brief.

Two things are worth stating plainly because they decide most of the
difference between bands. The first is spread of writing dates: eleven entries
dated across eleven weeks read differently from eleven entries dated across
four days, and the difference is visible. The second is whether the entries
change. An entry from week 9 that could have been written in week 1 suggests
the course has not reached the journal yet.
