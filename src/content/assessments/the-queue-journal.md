## The brief

> Keep a record of the queues you stood in. One a week, for eleven weeks,
> written the day it happened.

This is the piece of work students are most sceptical about in week 1 and most
attached to in week 12. It is a small ask, deliberately. One queue a week, a
hundred words or so, written while you still remember it.

What it is really for is the change in what you notice. The week 1 entries are
almost always about how long the wait was and how annoying it was. By week 5
they start naming the discipline. By week 8 people are writing about the sign
that told them the wait was fifteen minutes and whether that sign was
honest. That drift is the thing being marked, and it cannot be faked in a
weekend because the vocabulary arrives on a schedule and your entries will
show whether you had it yet.

Entries can be about your own queue, but most should not be. The point is the
ones you did not choose. A queue you were stuck in at an airport, a phone
queue, the line for a toilet at half time, the wait for a reply to an email
you needed answered.

## What you submit

One document, entries in week order, each headed with its week number and the
date you wrote it. No entry for week 12; that week you are presenting.

## A note on honesty

An entry you forgot to write is worth more as a gap than as a reconstruction.
Write "week 6, missed" and move on. This is marked on the habit, and the habit
includes admitting the weeks it lapsed.
