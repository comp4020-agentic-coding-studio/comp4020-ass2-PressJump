## The brief

> Every queue discipline is a claim about whose time is worth less. Find a
> queue, name the claim it makes, and argue about whether it should.

By week 8 you have the machinery. You know how to measure a wait, you know
what priority does to the people who do not have it, and you have seen what
happens when the queue is a list nobody publishes. This piece asks you to
stop measuring and take a position.

Some queues make the claim openly. A boarding group printed on a ticket says
exactly what it means. A triage category says it too, and defends itself, and
the defence is a good one. Others make it quietly. A phone line that is open
between nine and four in the afternoon has decided something about shift
workers without ever writing it down, and an appointment system that only
takes bookings online has decided something else.

You are not required to conclude that the queue is unjust. Several of the
queues students bring to this assignment are doing the right thing for
defensible reasons, and saying so with evidence is a stronger answer than a
complaint. What you are required to do is find the decision, show who it
costs, and put a number on it if a number exists.

## What you submit

An essay of 1200 to 1800 words. No abstract, no executive summary.

One structural requirement, and it is marked at 30 percent so take it
seriously. Before you make your own case, state the strongest version of the
argument against you, in the words its own defenders would use. Not a weak
version you can knock down. If the queue you are attacking is run by people
with reasons, those reasons go on the page.
