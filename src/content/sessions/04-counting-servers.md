Monday said pooling wins. This session is a walk, and the question at every
stop is how many servers there are, which turns out to be much harder than it
sounds.

## What happens

We walk together to four queues on or near campus. The library desk, the
campus pharmacy, the largest supermarket within ten minutes, and the bus
interchange. At each one, ten minutes of looking and ten minutes of arguing.

Three questions at every stop.

1. How many servers does this queue have. Not how many counters, how many
   servers. A counter with a trainee being supervised is a fraction of a
   server. A self-service machine that fails one time in eight is another
   fraction, and the fraction is not obvious.
2. Is it pooled, and if not, which of the five reasons applies.
3. If you pooled it tomorrow, what would break.

## The supermarket

We spend longest here because it is the best case against pooling and students
usually arrive convinced it is simply badly designed. Look for the space, the
impulse shelving, the express lane threshold, and the fact that the tills are
not interchangeable.

## Weather

We walk in anything short of a storm. If it is cancelled you will know by
09:00.

## Bring

- Shoes you can walk forty minutes in.
- A notebook. No stopwatch needed today; this session is about looking.
- Your five reasons not to pool, from Monday, written down.

## What leaves the room

One page per queue with a server count you can defend, a pooled or not-pooled
classification, and the reason. Two of these will contradict somebody else's
and that argument is where the marks in The Redesign come from.
