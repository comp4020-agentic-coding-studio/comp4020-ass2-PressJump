Marta is here for this one, which makes it the only session with somebody in
the room who has sat on the other side of the request you are writing.

## What happens

**Thirty minutes.** Pick an invisible queue. It cannot be yours; yours is
visible, that was the point of week 1. Past years have used the campus
counselling waitlist, a state housing register, a visa subclass, a specialist
referral pathway and the university's own complaints process.

**Forty minutes, drafting.** You write a request for three numbers. Current
queue length, arrival rate, median time to service. Marta reads them over your
shoulder and tells you which ones will be refused and why, which is usually
because you asked for a document rather than a figure, or because you asked a
question that requires the office to do analysis rather than look something
up.

**Forty minutes, estimating anyway.** Assume the request fails. Build an
estimate from the outside using what is already public. Annual reports,
budget documents, staffing numbers, a known service rate and Little's Law
running backwards.

**Ten minutes.** Compare the two numbers you now have for the same queue.

## Whether to actually send it

Your choice, and several students do. If you send it, say so in Whose Time It
Was and report what came back, including silence. Do not send anything to an
individual, and do not send anything on behalf of the course.

## Bring

- A laptop.
- One invisible queue you have a real reason to care about. This session goes
  much better when the queue matters to you.

## What leaves the room

A drafted request, an outside estimate of the same three quantities with the
working shown, and Marta's note on whether the request would land.
