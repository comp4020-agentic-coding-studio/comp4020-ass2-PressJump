Priority stops being a diagram this week. You go and watch it happen.

## What happens

**Twenty minutes, in the room.** The three categories and how to tell them
apart quickly, because you will have about two seconds to classify each one.

- **Sanctioned.** The system did it. A priority lane, a staff member calling
  someone forward, a card that says so.
- **Negotiated.** The people in the queue did it. Someone asked, someone let
  them, someone holding one item was waved ahead.
- **Unnoticed.** Somebody was served out of order and nobody reacted,
  including the person they went in front of.

**Eighty minutes, at your own queue.** One hour of recording, and the rest is
travel. Every out-of-order service, with a timestamp, a category and an
estimate of how many people were behind them.

**Twenty minutes, back in the room, next week if the timing does not work.**

## The number to compute

Queue jumps are cheap individually and this surprises people. One person
moving forward past eight people who each lose the jumper's service time is a
total cost you can calculate in seconds. Do it. Then think about whether the
smallness of that number is a defence or a distraction, because Whose Time It
Was is going to ask you.

## A note on ethics

You are observing a public space, counting events, not recording individuals.
Do not photograph anybody, do not follow anybody, do not record conversations,
and do not intervene. If a member of staff asks what you are doing, tell them
honestly; every student who has done so has been allowed to continue.

## Bring

- Observation sheet with the three categories as columns.
- A stopwatch.
- The name of a staff member to ask for, if your site has one.

## What leaves the room

One hour of jump records from your own queue, classified, with a total cost in
person-seconds and a one-line note on whether anybody objected.
