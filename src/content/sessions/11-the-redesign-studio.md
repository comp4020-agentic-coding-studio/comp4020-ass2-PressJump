Nine days before The Redesign is due. This is the session that makes the
difference between a good mark and a bad one, and attendance is close to
universal for a reason.

## What happens

Rotating groups of four. Everyone pitches once and critiques three times.

**Five minutes to pitch.** Your queue, your one change, your predicted effect
on the mean wait, and the arithmetic that got you there. The arithmetic has to
be visible; a claim without working is stopped at the thirty second mark and
you start again.

**Ten minutes of attack.** The other three have one job, which is to find
somebody your change makes worse. Not to be encouraging. Every change moves
waiting rather than removing it, week 11 said so, and if the room cannot find
the loser then either the change does nothing or you have not looked hard
enough.

Repeat four times, so everyone pitches once and attacks three times.

## What tends to happen

About a third of students change their redesign in this session. That is a
success, not a failure, and it is why this is scheduled nine days out rather
than two.

The most common fix is narrowing. People arrive with a programme of three
changes and leave with one, because the room cannot do the arithmetic on
three and neither can they.

## Bring

- Your week 1 numbers.
- Your predicted effect, with the working, on one page. Legible to somebody
  else, because somebody else is going to read it.
- Your best current answer to who it costs, so the room has something to
  improve rather than something to invent.

## What leaves the room

A change you can still defend after three people tried to break it, a named
group who waits longer, and in about a third of cases a different change from
the one you walked in with.
