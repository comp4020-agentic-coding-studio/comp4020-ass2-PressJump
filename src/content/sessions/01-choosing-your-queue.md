The most consequential two hours of the semester, which is a strange thing to
say about week 1. The queue you choose here is the queue in The Measured
Queue, in most of Whose Time It Was, and in The Redesign. Choosing badly costs
you the whole semester and it is very easy to do.

## What happens

**First forty minutes, in the room.** Everyone proposes a queue. We go round,
and Priya says why each one will not work. This sounds unkind and it is the
most useful part. The usual failures are a queue that only exists at 8am on a
Tuesday, a queue you cannot legally stand next to, a queue with three
customers an hour, and a queue that is really four queues.

**Next hour, outside.** You go and stand next to your candidate. Not to
measure it, just to look at it. Where does it start. Can you see the server.
Is there somewhere you can stand for an hour without being asked to move.

**Last twenty minutes, back in the room.** You write your queue on the board
with its location and its busy period. It is now yours.

## What makes a good queue for this course

- You can get to it twice in a week without it being a trip.
- At least thirty arrivals an hour during its busy period.
- You can see both the joining point and the service point from one position.
- Somebody is in charge of it and could in principle be asked about it.

## On permission

Most public queues need none. Anything on private property, anything indoors
with staff, and anything where you will be visibly writing things down for an
hour needs a conversation. Have it this week, not in week 4. Priya has a
one-paragraph email that works.

## Bring

- A phone with a stopwatch.
- Something to write on that works standing up. A phone is worse for this than
  a notebook and you will find out why on Wednesday next week.
- A second choice of queue, because roughly a third of first choices fail.

## What leaves the room

Your queue, written on the board and in the shared list, with its location and
busy period. It does not change after week 2 without talking to Tessa.
